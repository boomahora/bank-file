# Prepare for a bank review

## What you need

The record, the sources folder and the last twelve months of fee invoices or billing
statements. If the invoices are missing, say so and produce the rest of the pack without the
fee comparison rather than estimating anything.

## The pack

**1. Charged against agreed.** For every pricing row in the record, compare what the invoices
show against what the source says was agreed. Produce a line per difference, with the invoice
month, the charged amount, the agreed amount and the quote agreeing it. Total the difference
over the period.

Only compare what the record has a sourced row for. Do not reconstruct a full fee analysis
from the invoices, which is a different job and needs the bank's own billing data.

**2. Promises with no evidence of delivery.** Every open promise row, with the date it was
made, the words it was made in and how long it has been outstanding. If something in
`sources/` suggests it was delivered, say which file.

**3. Deadlines in the next two quarters.** From the derived dates work. Notice dates first,
then renewals.

**4. Waivers and concessions about to lapse.** Anything whose next-look date falls inside the
next two quarters, which is the leverage you have while you still have it.

**5. Open items from the last review.** What was agreed to be fixed, and whether anything in
`sources/` shows it was.

**6. Questions to ask.** Built from the **Not stated** list: the things the sources do not
answer. This is usually the most useful page in the pack.

## Rules

Every line carries its quote and its filename. The pack may go across the table from the
bank, so a line that cannot be sourced has to come out.

Where the record has a conflict between two documents, put it in the pack as a question
rather than as a finding.

Do not write recommendations on what to negotiate. The pack states what was agreed and what
happened. The judgment belongs to the person in the meeting.
